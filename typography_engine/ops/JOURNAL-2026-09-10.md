# 2026-09-10 — the v2 pet engine, checkpointed

13 commits, `366ed91` → `c835642`, on `claude/pet-engine-v2` (branched from `ops-consolidate`).
Tagged `checkpoint-2026-09-10`. Running on staging only, behind `PET_ENGINE=v2`; no live brand
has it. Every number below was measured, not estimated.

---

## What it is

A second pet renderer, `app/pet_v2/`, reached from the same call sites as the first through
`pet_proto.render_pet_portrait_dispatch`. With `PET_ENGINE` unset or anything but `v2`, the
first engine runs and the live brands are byte-identical to before this branch existed.

The animal is constructed from typography rather than a photograph with text laid over it:
streamlines follow the fur direction; each eye, the nose leather, the philtrum, the nostrils
and the mouth crease are drawn by dedicated passes from what is actually in the photo; the
silhouette sheds flyaway hairs; every remaining gap is filled down to the glyph floor. Letters
carry the photo's own colour; opacity carries tone. A type-only likeness score (face-weighted
SSIM of the blurred typography against the blurred source, no photo underneath) is printed
per render and was the judge for every change.

## The claim, and the numbers behind it

`PET_V2_VERBOSE=1` prints a `CLAIM METRICS` line per render. On the staging senior-dog upload:

    footprint 85.6% of the animal · exposed space 0.9% · 10,618 words
    letter-body collisions 1.08% · type-only likeness 0.572

On the four development photos at 1050px (`c835642`): black lab 0.618, tan dog 0.459,
doodle 0.408, cat 0.401; collisions 0.6–0.9%; exposed space 1.8–2.8%.

## What was found

**One size per streamline hid the whole hierarchy.** Each line took the average of the size
field over its full length. On a close-up a line runs cheek to frame edge, so every line
averaged to the same value: median 6px in every distance band. Words are now sized at their own
position. `945eb5a`.

**The "keep it fine" zone was the likeness weight map.** Its face ellipse is 1.8 eye-separations
wide. On a close-up it covered half the animal and 80% of structural words. Replaced by an
anatomical zone: eye discs, the fitted nose leather, a mouth band, with a short ramp. `945eb5a`,
widened for the eyes in `c835642`.

**Lanes were spaced for fine type.** Larger words on the far body collided and were rejected;
6px channel letters took the ground (120 structural words against 901 channel letters in the
far band). Lane spacing now floors at 0.85x the local type size. `945eb5a`.

**Distance from the face was distance from between the eyes.** On a close-up the nose is a full
eye-separation from that point, so the muzzle got the largest words in the frame. Distance is
now to the nearest feature, eye or nose, split into an anatomical near part (eye-separation
units) and a frame-relative far part. `c835642`.

**The engine kept per-render state in module globals.** Two renders overlapping on the service
threads shared one placement log; staging surfaced it to the customer as
`boolean index did not match indexed array ... 8767 vs 8761`. All per-render state is now
thread-local. Concurrent renders verified byte-identical to sequential. `a373443`.

**The backdrop choice was accepted and ignored**, and every request was a full render: preview,
loupe and each backdrop toggle overlapped and the third passed the browser's 120s limit. Now one
render per (photo, words, aspect) at the loupe height, cached; the loupe is a resize, a
backdrop toggle is arithmetic on the outer-ground weight, a duplicate request waits for the
render in flight. Measured: Gallery Gray 0.1s, print 64s, preview after print 0.1s. `12585cc`.

**Curvature-adaptive word length failed twice** (one long word dominated). Reverted to plain
round-robin; noted in the code.

### Fixes, each measured on a stand-in before it was deployed

**Silhouette glow, `0322f32` + `6dd2e21`.** Three causes. Edge pixels are part background
and were revealed through the letters and averaged into the gap colour: each pixel inside
the outline is now projected onto the line from local deep fur to local background and the
background share removed, in LAB chroma only (an RGB version turned the collie's white paws
violet; a luminance-weighted version read every lighter patch of fur as background). The
fringe hairs were fabricated: on every development photo the thin-line residual just outside
the matte is under 3 at the 75th percentile, so the 40–80 hairs per render had no evidence;
they are drawn only where a line really leaves the outline. Whisker spokes walked past the
outline on grass texture; outside the outline a spoke needs a stronger, unbroken line.
Stand-in: the tan dog with a deliberately loosened matte, inner-ring green shift −8.0 →
−0.7 a-units. Gate: eight pass, shepherd −0.0102 likeness against a 0.010 limit. Judged from
the picture as the metric losing credit for invented hairs, not a regression; recorded here
rather than by moving the threshold.

**Local coat mode, `b1466a0`.** One tonal mode per photo fails any two-tone or shadowed coat.
The coat luminance is read per pixel (masked blur, 1.5 base) and the mode chosen per region,
all three floor rules blended with soft weights. Stand-in: the tan dog with one half darkened
to black and the other lifted, which puts the mean where the collie's is. Black half, letters
vs nearby gaps: L 29 vs 9 with 27% of letters invisible → 49 vs 26 with 3%. Gate: nine pass,
every number identical (ink untouched). Pictures: the collie's black half is typography; the
senior's shadowed side carries words.

### 2026-09-15: a person or two pets on the pet site

Production went to `947b0c6` after the soak. Before it did, the question was what the engine
does with a human, or with two pets. Read from the code, then measured.

**What it did.** The page sends the pet flag for every upload, so there is no species check
and the human brands' "we couldn't find a face" gate is skipped on purpose. A person is matted
cleanly, the landmark model (cats and dogs only) declines, the dark-blob eye finder pairs
whatever it pairs, and the customer is told nothing. Two pets: the landmark model took the
largest box and one face; the second pet got no eye or nose pass, and because type grows with
distance from the one face, the largest words in the frame landed across its eyes (measured
on a two-lab composite: "LOYAL" and "BEAR" across the second muzzle, both its eyes dull). One
correction to what I first wrote: a second pet standing apart was NOT painted over. The
matte's largest-component rule governs only the solid fill; the soft matte is kept, so the
second animal stayed, minus the fill that keeps a light coat from dropping out.

**The warning.** The detector now runs on its own (`pet_landmarks.detect_subjects`), and the
entry point reports what it saw through a `notices` list that main.py turns into an
input-stage warning, the channel the page already shows under the preview: no cat or dog
("we couldn't spot a cat or dog in this photo, so the eyes and nose are our best guess"); a
pet whose face the model could not read; two pets with only one face read. Nothing is said
when the detector could not run. Notices are cached with the render, so the backdrop toggle
says the same thing the first render did.

**Two pets.** `comparable_subjects` keeps every box at least a quarter of the largest's area
(`PET_LM_SECOND_MIN`); each gets its own pose reading (`all_face_landmarks`). The landmark
string carries further faces after "|". In the engine every further face gets the eye, nose,
mouth and muzzle passes at its own eye separation, its own fine zone, its own eye and nose
reveal, its own whisker region clipped to its own eye line, the same weight in the likeness
map and the ink-priority map, and a place in the feature-distance rule, so type is fine on
both faces and grows away from both. The smallest face sets the working resolution. The
matte is solidified with every subject's box in hand, so a second pet standing apart gets the
solid fill too; with one subject `keep_boxes` is None and the matte is the one the gate was
measured on. The first face remains the primary for hero placement and the region map.

**A bug the composite found.** The first two-face render left an 11% bare patch beside the
second dog: streamline growth is one seed and its descendants, and it crosses a narrow bridge
(two chests touching) only if a seed happens to land on it -- one landmark configuration
crossed, the other did not -- and it can never reach a pet standing apart. Growth now re-seeds
any unreached territory a good three lanes from everything grown so far, for the
portrait-wide call only (the pocket, channel and micro-fill callers keep their single wave).
A single pet never has such territory: the hero dog through the site path and the lab
through the engine with landmarks are byte-identical before and after every change here.

### 2026-09-15, 03:04: production went down, and why

`947b0c6` was promoted to pawsinwords after the soak. The first customer-style render timed
out in the browser, the retry got a network error, ssh timed out, and the box needed a
restart from the provider's panel. The previous boot's journal has the cause: the kernel
killed a render worker at 6.8 GB (Sep 14 22:41), 6.9 GB (22:45) and 5.4 GB (Sep 15 03:04),
each time with the 6 GB of swap exhausted. The first two are during the staging soak, which
reported 166 requests and no failures: each kill fell between requests and the container
restarted before the next one arrived. The soak measured time and never looked at memory.
The box has 8 GB and runs five engine containers, which idle at 4 GB together. Neither the
memory of a v2 render nor the memory of the box was checked before promoting. Both are mine.
Production is back on the old engine (`PET_ENGINE=v2` removed from its `.env`); staging is
down.

**Measured here, one 1600px preview, the hero dog:** peak 2.9 GB resident, and 2.1 GB still
held after the render returned with the caches at 3 MB and the Python heap at 31 MB. The
held memory is glibc: its mmap threshold grows as large arrays come and go, after which the
full-frame float arrays land on the heap and freed heap is never returned; `malloc_trim`
gave back 1.1 GB on demand. The peak is the composite stage: at "tonal match" 1.7 GB of
arrays were alive, two dozen full-frame colour planes from stages long finished, all held
by name in one function until it returned.

**Three changes, ink byte-identical (hero dog through the site path, lab through the engine
with landmarks, same hashes as before):**

- Every composite-stage array is released the line after its last reader (`del`; nothing
  computed differently). `edge` was computed for a blend whose weight has been 0.0 for
  weeks; it is released on the spot.
- The image sets `MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=65536` (fixed, so every array over 64 KB is its own
  mapping and returns to the OS when freed), `MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_` and
  `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2`; the entry point calls `malloc_trim(0)` after each render.
- A memory cap on the container. A render that outgrows it kills that worker, which
  restarts in seconds; it can no longer take the box down. First put in the brand compose
  files, which turned out not to be what runs: every tree is started by `promote.sh` from
  the one `docker-compose.yml`, with the project, container name, port and image coming
  from the tree's `.env` (the brand files serve only the `./staging.sh`-style wrappers,
  which is why their `ps` printed an empty table). The cap lives in `docker-compose.yml`
  as `CONTAINER_MEM_LIMIT`, set to `3500m` in the staging and pawsinwords `.env`; unset,
  the default is above the box's RAM and the human-brand trees run as before. This
  Compose (v5.1.4) honored only the `deploy.resources.limits` form, so it is declared
  both ways. Checked with `docker inspect --format '{{.HostConfig.Memory}}'`.

## 2026-09-18: Lifelike specks and the magenta eye, one cause

The two Lifelike defects on the plan (blue and green specks along the cheek of
10-smile; magenta band and black blob over the shadowed eye of 06-sidelight) reproduce
locally with the production engine.env and the ISNet model, and are one bug. The
polarity shadow model in displacement.py keeps the photo's hue as channel / luminance
and re-maps brightness. In deep shadow a JPEG pixel is (5, 0, 0): luminance under 1,
so the division turns four levels of compression noise into pure blue at full
strength (measured at the wild pixels: source luminance p50 1.3, p90 4.2; chroma p50
4). Vibrance then makes it (68, 68, 255). Stage dumps: the base and ink_col layers are
clean; the blue is present at A-after-ink-branch, i.e. it is made in the polarity block.

Fix: a pedestal on both sides of the division, `(bgr + p) / (luma + p)`,
`TYPO_POLARITY_PEDESTAL` default 8 (0 restores the old math). A pixel with no light
comes out neutral; skin at luminance 140 moves under 1%; a navy blouse at 10 keeps
three quarters of its colour. Measured on the two renders (900 px previews):

    pixels with chroma > 120     pedestal 0   8    16
