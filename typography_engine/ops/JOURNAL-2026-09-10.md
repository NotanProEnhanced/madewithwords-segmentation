13 commits, `366ed91` → `c835642`, on `claude/pet-engine-v2` (branched from `ops-consolidate`).
Tagged `checkpoint-2026-09-10`. Running on staging only, behind `PET_ENGINE=v2`; no live brand
has it. Every number below was measured, not estimated.

## What it is

A second pet renderer, `app/pet_v2/`, reached from the same call sites as the first through
`pet_proto.render_pet_portrait_dispatch`. With `PET_ENGINE` unset or anything but `v2`, the
first engine runs and the live brands are byte-identical to before this branch existed.

The animal is constructed from typography rather than a photograph with text laid over it:
streamlines follow the fur direction; each eye, the nose leather, the philtrum, the nostrils
and the mouth crease are drawn by dedicated passes from what is actually in the photo; the
silhouette sheds flyaway hairs; every remaining gap is filled down to the glyph floor. Letters
carry the photo's own colour; opacity carries tone. A type-only likeness score (face-weighted
SSIM of the blurred typography against the blurred source, no photo underneath) is printed
per render and was the judge for every change.

## The claim, and the numbers behind it

`PET_V2_VERBOSE=1` prints a `CLAIM METRICS` line per render. On the staging senior-dog upload:

    footprint 85.6% of the animal · exposed space 0.9% · 10,618 words
    letter-body collisions 1.08% · type-only likeness 0.572

On the four development photos at 1050px (`c835642`): black lab 0.618, tan dog 0.459,
doodle 0.408, cat 0.401; collisions 0.6–0.9%; exposed space 1.8–2.8%.

## What was found

**One size per streamline hid the whole hierarchy.** Each line took the average of the size
field over its full length. On a close-up a line runs cheek to frame edge, so every line
averaged to the same value: median 6px in every distance band. Words are now sized at their own
position. `945eb5a`.

**The "keep it fine" zone was the likeness weight map.** Its face ellipse is 1.8 eye-separations
wide. On a close-up it covered half the animal and 80% of structural words. Replaced by an
anatomical zone: eye discs, the fitted nose leather, a mouth band, with a short ramp. `945eb5a`,
widened for the eyes in `c835642`.

**Lanes were spaced for fine type.** Larger words on the far body collided and were rejected;
6px channel letters took the ground (120 structural words against 901 channel letters in the
far band). Lane spacing now floors at 0.85x the local type size. `945eb5a`.

**Distance from the face was distance from between the eyes.** On a close-up the nose is a full
eye-separation from that point, so the muzzle got the largest words in the frame. Distance is
now to the nearest feature, eye or nose, split into an anatomical near part (eye-separation
units) and a frame-relative far part. `c835642`.

**The engine kept per-render state in module globals.** Two renders overlapping on the service
threads shared one placement log; staging surfaced it to the customer as
`boolean index did not match indexed array ... 8767 vs 8761`. All per-render state is now
thread-local. Concurrent renders verified byte-identical to sequential. `a373443`.

**The backdrop choice was accepted and ignored**, and every request was a full render: preview,
loupe and each backdrop toggle overlapped and the third passed the browser's 120s limit. Now one
render per (photo, words, aspect) at the loupe height, cached; the loupe is a resize, a
backdrop toggle is arithmetic on the outer-ground weight, a duplicate request waits for the
render in flight. Measured: Gallery Gray 0.1s, print 64s, preview after print 0.1s. `12585cc`.

**Curvature-adaptive word length failed twice** (one long word dominated). Reverted to plain
round-robin; noted in the code.

   behind the "preparing files" state.

## Afternoon: the gate, and three fixes through it

`dc242ea` → `b1466a0`, tagged `checkpoint-2026-09-10b`. Staging is on `b1466a0`.

### The pet set exists

Nine photos at `/root/typortrait-testset/pets/src/` (tabby, doodle, collie, samoyed, old dog,
corgi, shepherd, black lab, senior close-up), chosen to vary framing as much as subject.
`ops/render-petset.sh` renders them through the container and files each render with the
engine's own report card; `ops/pet-gate.sh` compares two runs and fails on a threshold. Both
verified against a local instance first. Two harness lessons cost an hour each and are now
built in: a run is named after the image the container is actually running, not the tree's
HEAD (a tree at one commit with a container still on another filed baseline renders under
the new name and overwrote the real ones), and an existing run is never overwritten.

Baseline `dc242ea`, likeness: lab 0.615, old dog 0.568, samoyed 0.475, doodle 0.453, corgi
0.450, senior 0.444, tabby 0.414, shepherd 0.337, collie 0.332. Footprint 83.5–85.5%,
exposed 0.5–1.0%, collisions 0.81–1.20%.

### What the baseline pictures showed

The two full-body photos were not sellable. A glow traced round the whole silhouette, green
on the collie and white-gold on the backlit shepherd; the collie's black half was empty of
type; the shepherd's face was a smudge. The senior close-up was close, with a shadowed side
that read as photo.

### Fixes, each measured on a stand-in before it was deployed

**Silhouette glow, `0322f32` + `6dd2e21`.** Three causes. Edge pixels are part background
and were revealed through the letters and averaged into the gap colour: each pixel inside
the outline is now projected onto the line from local deep fur to local background and the
background share removed, in LAB chroma only (an RGB version turned the collie's white paws
violet; a luminance-weighted version read every lighter patch of fur as background). The
fringe hairs were fabricated: on every development photo the thin-line residual just outside
the matte is under 3 at the 75th percentile, so the 40–80 hairs per render had no evidence;
they are drawn only where a line really leaves the outline. Whisker spokes walked past the
outline on grass texture; outside the outline a spoke needs a stronger, unbroken line.
Stand-in: the tan dog with a deliberately loosened matte, inner-ring green shift −8.0 →
−0.7 a-units. Gate: eight pass, shepherd −0.0102 likeness against a 0.010 limit. Judged from
the picture as the metric losing credit for invented hairs, not a regression; recorded here
rather than by moving the threshold.

**Local coat mode, `b1466a0`.** One tonal mode per photo fails any two-tone or shadowed coat.
The coat luminance is read per pixel (masked blur, 1.5 base) and the mode chosen per region,
all three floor rules blended with soft weights. Stand-in: the tan dog with one half darkened
to black and the other lifted, which puts the mean where the collie's is. Black half, letters
vs nearby gaps: L 29 vs 9 with 27% of letters invisible → 49 vs 26 with 3%. Gate: nine pass,
every number identical (ink untouched). Pictures: the collie's black half is typography; the
senior's shadowed side carries words.

### What remains, in order

1. Resolution follows face size: full-body photos render with the face at a few hundred
   pixels and every feature pass at the six-pixel floor. Choose the working height so the
   eyes get enough pixels, up to the print cap; expect roughly double the render time on the
   shepherd. Collie, shepherd and corgi are the test cases.
2. Hero placement: a silhouette-distance term, so the name does not land across the outline
   (senior, bottom left).
3. Shepherd collisions sit at 1.01–1.20% against a 1.20% limit; the smallest subject at the
   font floor. Item 1 may resolve it.
4. Production promote after a soak: `PET_ENGINE=v2` in the pawsinwords tree's `.env`.

## 2026-09-14: faces on full-body photos, and the build to soak

`b1466a0` → `4dce903`, tagged `checkpoint-2026-09-14`. Staging is on `4dce903`. Gate chain:
`dc242ea` → `6dd2e21` → `b1466a0` → `8383eaf` → `4dce903`, every step rendered and compared.

### What the full-body pictures needed

The collie, shepherd and corgi had faces of two dots and a smudge. A stand-in (the tan dog at
45% in a frame of its own blurred background) showed the eye detector taking the ear tips as
eyes, and a fix went in for that (`332905c`: an eye-separation prior and a size cap in the
pair scorer, detector scales from the head band's width, and a re-render up to the print cap
when the eyes are under 170px apart). Deployed, it changed nothing: all nine renders were
byte-identical to `b1466a0`. The probe log showed why: on the real shepherd the heuristic
paired two dark patches on the shoulder and chest, 236px apart and 98px different in height.
It has no notion of where the head is on a full-body photo, and no stand-in built from a
head-and-shoulders crop could have shown that.

The fix was already in the repo. `pet_landmarks.py` (YOLOX + RTMPose AP-10K), verified on
every framing when it was written, had never been connected to v2. It now runs in the site's
entry point and hands eyes and nose to the engine (`8383eaf`). Shepherd: eyes level, 131px
apart, nose below; the 1.30x re-render fired; likeness 0.327 → 0.425. Eight of nine photos
moved up (lab 0.620 → 0.670), collisions fell on every one. The old dog fell 0.568 → 0.496 and
failed the gate: its landmarks were right, the score's anchors had moved, and the picture is
the best render of the whole effort. Recorded as a metric artefact; from here the anchors are
stable and the likeness column means what it says.

### Two more, then the soak build

Hero placement (`4dce903`): a hero line must clear the silhouette by the hero type's height.
Tan dog: 2 of 3 hero words crossed the outline → 1, 116px inside.

Determinism (`4dce903`): the shepherd rendered with 4,891 words in the gated run and 4,882 in
a probe. The shared glyph cache stored a bitmap built from the first call's exact angle and
opacity and reused it for every call in the same bin, so output depended on which photos had
warmed the cache (tan dog cold vs warm: 2705 vs 2769 words). Bitmaps are built from the bin
values now; cold and warm are byte-identical. Gate `8383eaf` → `4dce903`: nine pass, likeness
within ±0.006.

### Both eyes alike, `c7d1720`

Reported from the staging renders: on the black lab and the collie one eye came out pale and
glassy, the other dim, though the lab's are lit alike. Two causes on the development lab with
a trusted landmark pair: the eye renderer and the eye reveal decided the eye's geometry
separately (one had the landmark prior, the other only the dark-blob fit), and the coat-mode
reading blurred each eye's own iris and glint into the luminance around it, so one eye's
surroundings crossed the mid-coat threshold and the other's did not (L 95 vs 27 at the same
0.96 reveal). One `eye_geometry` decision for both; the eyes and nose left out of the coat
reading; half the photo's own tone kept inside the eye reveal. Staging: lab, collie and old
dog all show two matched eyes. `c7d1720` is the soak build.

### The soak, `0283d90` + `PIXEL_BUDGET`

Local, through the site's entry point, 38 cases: every slider, backdrop and aspect; a 240px
upload and a 3600x4500 one; greyscale, rotated, panorama, all-white, all-black, noise, PNG
with alpha, a room with no pet, a small face, a two-tone coat; empty words, one letter, fifty
words, a 36-letter word, non-ASCII, punctuation, lowercase; three photos at once and the same
photo three times at once. 37 pass. The one failure is garbage bytes, which raises the
ValueError the site turns into a 400 -- correct. Two findings:

- A 2400x700 panorama took 146 s for the preview: the working height alone let the padded
  4:5 canvas grow to 5,500 x 6,900px. The working resolution now carries a pixel budget as
  well as a height (a 4:5 canvas at that height); an ordinary portrait is untouched, the
  panorama renders in 68 s.
- The 2400px print took 98 s here against 64 s a week ago. It runs off the request now, so
  no customer waits on it, but the engine has grown heavier and this is worth a profile.

Staging: `ops/soak-petset.sh` runs the same matrix over the nine-photo set on the container,
with the concurrent cases, and lists failures, slow renders and threshold breaches.

### Speed, `947b0c6`

Profiled a 1600px preview: 706,000 scalar numpy clamps in the per-word and per-point closures
(9 s), 21,926 text bitmaps rendered for ~6,700 distinct word/size/alpha triples, and 256
quantiles of a 2-3 million pixel layer taken 21 times inside the tonal-match bisection
(3.4 s). Plain min/max; the unrotated text cached per triple; a fixed-stride subsample for
the quantiles. Engine A/B on one box: 43.0 → 31.5 s, ink byte-identical. Staging: tabby 46 → 31
s, doodle 50 → 34, collie 31 → 22. Gate `c7d1720` → `947b0c6`: nine moved by the quantile
table, every number identical. Staging soak (QUICK): 22 requests, none failed; two previews
at once share the box's cores and each take twice as long (100 s), which is capacity: on a
two-core box a render concurrency of one serves the first customer in 50 s instead of both in
100. `947b0c6` is the soak build.

### The full soak, `947b0c6` on staging

Nine photos x three slider settings x three aspects x two backdrops, plus the concurrent cases:
166 requests, none failed, none over 90 s, exposed space under 3.0% everywhere. Fresh renders
18.5 / 27.8 / 66 s (min / median / max, the 66 s the second of two at once). Every backdrop
toggle came from the cache in about a second. Capacity checked: the box has two cores and
`TYPO_RENDER_CONCURRENCY` is unset in both trees, so each container already queues renders one
at a time; nothing to set, and a soak on staging shares cores with production.

One finding. Letter-body collisions at the site's default (Small, 0.30) stay under the 1.20%
claim line on all 54 renders. At Medium two of 54 cross it (1.22, 1.40), at Large eight of 27
(1.23 to 1.70, the lab on a landscape canvas the worst). All on Gallery Dark, since the mid
backdrop reuses the same ink. Bigger words on the same footprint collide more; the collision
cap in the placer is per word and does not tighten with the size slider. Not a broken picture,
half a percent of touching letters, and a customer chooses Large; but it is the one row of the
matrix the claim does not yet hold for. Open item: make the placer's overlap cap scale with
the slider, then gate Large the way Small is gated. Production goes to `947b0c6`.
